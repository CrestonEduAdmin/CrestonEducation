Asked before you ask

The questions candidates actually send me

Can I sit Part I while still in residency?

Yes, and it is usually the right call. Part I needs a primary medical qualification and anaesthesia training, not a completed one — and a pass does not expire. Sitting it during residency means you are revising basic sciences while you are still surrounded by them, and it takes the pressure off the year you are trying to finish everything else.

How long do I need to prepare?

For most working residents, four to six focused months. What matters far more than the number of months is whether your revision is question-shaped from the beginning. Candidates who read for three months and then start questions in the fourth almost always underperform against candidates who did both from week one.

I have failed Part I before. Is this useful?

It is the case where structured coaching helps most. ESAIC gives you a performance report broken down by paper and subject area. That report, not a textbook, is where the next attempt should start — so retaking candidates are placed into the modules their report actually points at, rather than restarting the syllabus from page one.

Should I do EDAIC or FRCA?

If your career is going to be inside the NHS, the FRCA is the road. If you are currently outside the UK, want a European-standard credential you can begin immediately without a UK post, or are aiming at continental Europe, the EDAIC is both more accessible and more portable — and it can exempt you from the Primary FRCA if you later change direction. Does the EDAIC let me work in the UK or the UAE?

No qualification does that on its own, and be careful of anyone who says otherwise. The UK requires GMC registration; the UAE requires licensing through the relevant emirate's authority, DataFlow verification and an assessment. What the diploma does is remove exam barriers, strengthen an application and make your specialist standing legible to people who do not know your national degree.

What if I cannot make the live sessions?

Every session is recorded, and assignments and model answers are released to the batch regardless of attendance. Because admissions roll and the content is modular, a module lost to a rota is one you pick up on its next run rather than a course you have dropped out of.
