The decision most people get stuck on

EDAIC, FRCA or FCAI

They are not competitors so much as different bets on where you will spend your career. The wrong question is which is hardest. The right question is which system you want to be inside in five years.

EDAICFRCAFCAI / FCARCSI
Awarded by ESAIC — the European Society of Anaesthesiology and Intensive Care Royal College of Anaesthetists, UK College of Anaesthesiologists of Ireland
Built for A pan-European standard, deliberately portable between countries UK training. Primary to enter higher training, Final before CCT Irish training, with strong international recognition
Where you sit it Many centres worldwide, several languages, no prior European experience needed Largely UK-based, in English Ireland and some international centres
Entry barrier Lowest. Part I needs only a primary medical qualification and anaesthesia training Higher. Realistically tied to a UK training post or registration Requires registration and a defined period of anaesthesia employment
Written format Multiple true/false — five independent statements per stem Mixed, including single best answer Mixed MCQ and written
Cross-recognition Accepted for exemption from the RCoA and CAI primary examinations Exempts you from EDAIC Part I Exempts you from EDAIC Part I
Strongest in Continental Europe, and as an international benchmark on any CV UK, Commonwealth, and much of the Gulf Ireland, UK, and international posts
Sensible if You are outside the UK now and want a European-standard credential you can start on immediately Your career is going to be built inside the NHS You are aiming at Irish training or the Irish route into Europe

Cross-recognition and exemption rules are set by the colleges and change. Confirm the current position with the RCoA, CAI and ESAIC before building a plan on it.
